# READING THE DASHBOARD

> Everything the page does for the person reading it. None of it needs explaining to use, but
> knowing it is there is the difference between reading a long document and navigating one.

---

## 1. Getting around

| | |
|---|---|
| **Sidebar** | Every document, grouped. Click one to open it; click the caret on the open one to show its sections. |
| **Section rail** | The sections of the open document, with the one you are reading marked. |
| **Previous / next** | At the foot of every document, in sidebar order. |
| **Top bar** | Appears once you scroll past the title, so you always know which document you are in. |
| `/` | Focus the search box. |
| `[` | Collapse the sidebar to a rail. |
| `Esc` | Close the full-size view, clear the search, or release a pinned diagram. |

The address bar tracks where you are (`#/2-diagrams/why-elk`), so a link to a specific section of a
specific document can be pasted to someone else — and the back button works.

## 2. Search

Filters the sidebar as you type, matching both document titles and section headings. A document
with matching sections opens itself so you can see them. It is a navigator, not a full-text
search — it looks at the titles, not the prose.

## <a id="folding"></a>3. Folding sections

Click any heading to fold its section away; click again to open it. The arrow in the margin does
the same thing.

What is folded is remembered per document, so a long reference page stays folded down to the parts
you actually use. Following a link into a folded section unfolds it — and every section above it —
on the way.

Selecting text inside a heading does not fold it, so you can still copy a title.

## <a id="full-size"></a>4. Diagrams full size

Click a diagram's border, or the **Expand** button in its corner, to open it full size. Then:

- **Drag** to pan, **scroll** to zoom, or use the `−` / `+` buttons.
- **Hover and click still work** — see [Diagrams](./2.%20DIAGRAMS.md#hovering-and-pinning). This is
  the combination that makes a wall-sized flowchart usable: zoom to the region, then pin the box
  you are following.
- `Esc` or **Close** to come back.

A diagram opens at a size that fits the window, but never so small it stops being legible — a very
tall flowchart opens readable and is panned rather than shrunk to nothing.

Clicking a box or an arrow inside a diagram pins it rather than opening the full-size view; that is
why the Expand button exists.

## <a id="comments"></a>5. Comments

Select any text in a document and a **Comment** button appears. The comment shows in the margin,
tied to the sentence it belongs to; hovering either the highlight or the card lights both, so the
link between the margin and the text is never a guess.

Some things worth knowing before relying on them:

- **They live in your browser, not in the repository.** Nobody else sees them, and they do not
  travel with the file. They are for reading a draft, not for reviewing one as a team.
- **They survive an edit.** A comment stores its quote plus a little context either side, so after
  the document is changed and rebuilt it finds its sentence again. If the sentence is gone, the
  comment is kept and marked *text not found* rather than thrown away.
- **They can be turned off** — for you, with the toggle at the foot of the sidebar; for a project,
  with `"comments": false` in [the config](./3.%20CONFIGURATION.md#settings), which removes the
  feature from the page entirely.
- **A browser that refuses storage loses them** when the page closes. The page says so the first
  time it happens rather than pretending to have saved.

When the margin is too narrow for a readable card, the cards shrink to numbered dots that open on
click, rather than overlapping the text.

## 6. Printing

The sidebar is dropped, every folded section is opened, and the fold arrows and heading anchors are
hidden. What prints is the document, not the interface.

## 7. What the page needs

Nothing. No server, no network, no install. Open `index.html` by double-clicking it.

If your browser refuses local storage — some do, on `file://` — the dashboard still works; it just
will not remember which document you had open, what you folded, or your comments.
